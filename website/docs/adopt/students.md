# Student guide

Welcome. This book teaches you how production-grade software gets built when AI
agents are part of the team — **principles first, tools second**, on the working
assumption that tools turn over much faster than principles do.

## Why read it

If you're a student or early-career engineer, the value here isn't a tutorial on
this week's hot tool. It's a **mental model and vocabulary** that will still make
sense in five years: what an AI-driven lifecycle is, why it's structured the way it
is, and what discipline it takes to ship software you can trust.

## How to read it

**Read the Preface first and the Glossary (Chapter 24) last.** In between, pick the
[reader path](../../../book/README.md#reader-paths) that matches where you are.

### If you're in your first or second year

Build vocabulary and intuition before depth:

1. **Ch 1 — Why an AI-Driven SDLC?** (the motivation)
2. **Ch 2 — Mental Models** (the lenses)
3. **Ch 3 — Federation of Lifecycles** (how engineering work is structured)
4. **Ch 10 — Agent Design** (read the *roles* only; skip the contracts first time)
5. **Ch 13 — Failure-First QA** (how quality actually works)
6. **Ch 23 — Final Principles** (the takeaways)

Then read the rest linearly when you're ready.

### If you're further along

Read linearly through Parts I–IV, then dip into Part V (operating) and the
**bridges** that connect to what you already know (Requirements, Architecture, Data,
Planning).

## How to study with it

- **Each chapter** opens with **learning objectives** (`LO N.1`, `LO N.2`, …).
  Treat them as a checklist: after the chapter, can you do each one?
- **Use the callouts.** *Key Idea* = the one thing to remember; *Common Pitfall* =
  the mistake to avoid; *Example* = make it concrete; *Atlas Learn* = see it play
  out in one running case study.
- **Do the exercises** at the end of each chapter — they're reflection and
  application prompts, not busywork.
- **Self-test** with the [test bank](../../../test-bank/): try the multiple-choice
  questions for a chapter and check yourself against the rationale.
- **Read the appendices** (Atlas Learn case studies) once you've met the concepts —
  they tie everything together.
